# ADR-022: Behavioural data is mirrored at attempt grain and aggregated at answer grain

**Status**: Accepted
**Date**: 2026-08-26
**Amends**: ADR-021 (the copy allowlist loses `question_result`, the profile allowlist gains it)
**Decided by**: Operator
**Evidence**: measured on the 2026-08-07 snapshot during the P1 behavioural import, 2026-08-26

## Context

P1 specified a complete row-for-row mirror of both behavioural tables. It was built and run, so this
decision is made against measurements rather than estimates.

**`source_results`** — 1,136,204 rows. Bounded by attempts, one row per quiz attempt, carries
`student_ref`, `total_points` and `attempt_index`. Cheap to hold and genuinely row-level: cohorts,
learning curves and the corrected totals behind the point-biserial all need it.

**`source_answers`** — 13,776,378 rows, 3.8 GB, one row per student per answered question. Three
things surfaced once it existed:

1. **Nothing in the program ever annotates one of those rows.** Every consumer named in the core
   plan (p-value, distractor distribution, point-biserial) reads a `GROUP BY` over them.
2. **The table is unbounded.** It grows with students × time, forever. A platform with 100× the
   students has 1.4 billion rows here, while the question bank — the part the Lab actually enriches —
   stays roughly the same size.
3. **The import was 59× slower than necessary**, for an unrelated reason: the upsert did a `SELECT`
   then an `INSERT`/`UPDATE` per row, two round-trips × 14.9M rows ≈ 40 hours.

Measured comparison, on the real data:

| Approach | Rows in the Lab | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Row-by-row upsert (as originally built) | 14.9M | ~40 h |
| Same semantics, batched `ON CONFLICT` | 14.9M | 22.8 min |
| Aggregate pushed down into MySQL | 307,382 | ~32 s |

The aggregate was cross-checked against the raw mirror before anything was deleted: item counts and
`n_correct` matched on all 27,946 questions with data, `p_value` agreed to floating-point epsilon,
and both stats tables reconcile to exactly 13,776,378 answer events.

## Decision

**Mirror what gets enriched and is bounded. Aggregate what is only ever counted and is unbounded.**

1. The nine bank tables and `source_results` remain row-for-row mirrors.
2. `source_answers` is **dropped**. `question_result` is never mirrored.
3. It is replaced by two derived tables computed by pushing the aggregation into the read-only
   source: **`source_item_stats`** (58,284 rows) and **`source_option_stats`** (249,098 rows). Both
   carry a `scope` discriminator — `active` (attempt not soft-deleted) and `all` — because 71% of
   `results` rows are soft-deleted and which basis a published statistic should use is P3's decision,
   not P1's.
4. These tables store `n`, `n_correct`, `p_value` and the corrected-total components `m1`, `m0`,
   `sd`. They do **not** store `r_pbis`: that is P3's headline metric and one arithmetic line over
   these columns, needing no raw rows. P1 stores only what genuinely requires the (attempt ×
   question) grain and would be lost when the raw rows go.
5. **`question_result` moves from `source_tables` to `profile_tables`** — readable as aggregates,
   never copyable. `SourceReader::assertCopyable('question_result')` now throws.
6. All mirror writes go through one batched write path (`BatchUpsert`), preserving idempotency, the
   inserted/updated/unchanged counters, `payload_hash` semantics and resume.

### Why the allowlist move is the load-bearing part

Without it, "the Lab does not store raw answer rows" is a convention that any future job could break
by accident. With it, the existing guard refuses by name, and a test asserts the refusal. This is the
same reasoning that made the 2026-08-23 split worth having: reading a count is not storing a row, and
the split is what makes the difference enforceable rather than aspirational.

## Consequences

- The Lab database drops from **4.3 GB to 673 MB**. The full behavioural pipeline runs in ~100 s
  rather than ~40 h; the whole bank re-imports in 8 s.
- **P3's contract changes.** It previously expected `source_answers` at the (attempt × question)
  grain. It now receives `source_results` plus the two stats tables. The core program plan and P1's
  spec are updated accordingly. P3 computes `r_pbis` from the stored components.
- **What is genuinely lost**: the ability to slice answer events by a dimension not anticipated here
  — a date window, first-attempts-only, a per-quiz split — *without re-running the pushdown*. This is
  a much smaller loss than it appears, and the next section says why.
- Never-chosen options are preserved. 45,840 of 124,549 options (37%) were chosen by nobody; the
  stats table is built from `source_question_options LEFT JOIN` the aggregate so they appear with
  `chosen_n = 0`. Building from the aggregate alone would have silently dropped exactly the rows the
  "dead distractor" rule exists to find.
- Questions with no answer data (1,328) get rows with `n = 0`, so "measured, nothing there" stays
  distinguishable from "never computed".

## On "reproducible from raw rows"

Constitution §V requires that every statistical output be reproducible from raw rows, proven by a
sample-based test. P3's own acceptance criterion repeats it. This decision does not weaken that
requirement, but it does change where the raw rows live, and that is worth stating plainly rather
than leaving implied.

The raw rows have not been destroyed. They are in the InjazEdu MySQL snapshot, on this machine,
frozen at 2026-08-07 and never refreshed — which constitution §III already treats as the Lab's
reproducibility base: *"Everything in the Lab database is reproducible — re-import from the snapshot,
re-run the pipeline."* The Lab simply no longer keeps a second copy of 13.8M rows it never reads
individually.

`StatsReproducibilityTest` therefore recomputes a sample of stored statistics **directly from
`question_result` and `results`**, in PHP, and requires agreement. It deliberately does not compare
one MySQL aggregate against another, because the failure mode it guards is one both aggregates would
share (see below).

Re-slicing costs one `GROUP BY`, measured at ~5 s. The objection to pre-aggregation is normally "you
have baked in the grain and cannot re-slice" — that objection does not apply when the source is a
frozen local snapshot and recomputation is free.

## The precision trap, recorded because it is silent

**MySQL quantizes to 4 decimal places** in both `AVG()` over an integer expression and in decimal
division (`div_precision_increment`, default 4). The naive form of the pushdown put a 5e-5 error on
18,586 of 27,814 questions — plausible-looking output, wrong in the 5th decimal, and identical across
two independently written MySQL aggregates. Only an explicit cast is exact:

```sql
CAST(SUM(qr.points > 0) AS DOUBLE) / COUNT(*)
```

Separately, **PostgreSQL renders `double precision` at fewer digits than PHP's `precision = 14`
parses back**, so a raw double written to the Lab returns differing in the 15th digit. Stored
statistics are therefore rounded to 10 decimal places before hashing and writing, which makes the
stored value, its `stats_hash`, and every later read exactly consistent. The largest magnitude in
these columns is ~132, so 10 decimals is at most 13 significant digits and round-trips exactly.

## Resume cursors and a live source

`resume_cursor` is id-based (`WHERE id > ?`). It sees appended rows and is structurally blind to
edits of rows already copied. Against a frozen snapshot that is correct and cheap. **A live source
would need a watermark on `(updated_at, id)`**, compared as a pair. Recorded here, not built: P1 has
no live source.

## Re-evaluation triggers

- A consumer appears that must annotate or retrieve an individual answer event.
- P3 needs a slice the stats tables cannot express and the pushdown cannot cheaply recompute.
- The source stops being a frozen local snapshot — at which point the reproducibility argument above
  needs re-examining along with the resume cursor.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep the raw mirror, batched.** Entirely viable *for this platform*: 7 minutes and 3.8 GB. Rejected
  because it is the one component that does not travel to a larger source, and because it costs real
  storage and import time for rows nothing reads individually.
- **Postgres FDW (`mysql_fdw`)** — query MySQL from Postgres, zero copy. Rejected on security: the
  read-only guarantee is three layers *inside Laravel*, and an FDW routes around all three, so a
  Postgres-side write could reach MySQL. Also new infrastructure (Principle I).
- **Store `r_pbis` as well.** Rejected as scope: `tasks.md` lists item statistics as P3's, and the
  coefficient is derivable from the stored components without raw rows. Storing its *inputs* is the
  part that must happen in P1.
- **Aggregate at a single scope.** Rejected: with 71% of attempts soft-deleted, picking one basis
  now would be guessing on P3's behalf. Two scopes cost a row each and no migration later.
